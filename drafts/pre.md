## Part 1

Good morning, everyone. Today our group is going to talk about AI-generated music and how artificial intelligence is changing music composition. 

Imagine typing a short prompt like ‘a relaxing piano song for a rainy night’ and getting a brand-new piece of music in just a few seconds. That is the basic idea behind AI-generated music. 

It is music that a computer system can compose, generate, or assist in creating by learning patterns from a large amount of existing music. 

This topic matters because AI is no longer only analyzing music—it is now helping create it.

 As a result, it is changing the way music can be produced, used, and understood. 

To understand this topic better, the next speaker will briefly introduce the technology behind AI music generation.

## Part 3

Key benefits of ai music composition

Today ill focus on three benefits of ai music composition: efficiency, cost, and customization.

Firstly, generative music systems can produce many musical drafts in seconds, which is perfect for brainstorming, by trying multiple chord progressions, melodies, or textures and keep the best. One empirical comparison found Ai music generation can have a clear advantage in compositional efficiency when rapid output is needed. Even when humans still do the final taste-check, AI often speeds up the “first 80%” of iteration.

Second, cost. For videos, games and ads, music budgets often include hiring composers, studio time, and listening. AI tools can help create background tracks on demand and scale production for lots of content. A CISAC-commissioned economic study forecasts that by 2028, generative AI will account for a major share of music-library revenues, partly because businesses seek scalable and lower-cost options for background music use cases.

And thirdly, AI music has high level of customization. Instead of one track for everyone, AI can generate versions which are tailored to moods, brand styles, or user context. For example, research on adaptive music for games describes systems that let players influence training style choices, enabling more personalized soundtracks. Real-time generative music projects also support interactive musical changes.

So overall, AI helps creators move faster, spend less, and fit music to the exact content.

## Part 5 Challenges, Concerns & Conclusion

First, **emotional depth**.
 AI can analyze thousands of sad songs and produce something that sounds sad. But does it *feel* sadness? No. A human composer writes from real heartbreak, joy, or loss. AI only mimics them. So for deeply emotional scenes human music still wins.

Second, **copyright and ownership**.
 If an AI generates a song, who owns it? The user? The AI company? Or no one? This is still a legal gray area. Besides , Some AI tools have been accused of learning from copyrighted songs without permission. So we need clear rules.

Third, **impact on human musicians**.
 Will AI replace composers? I don’t think so – not completely. Repetitive, low-budget background music? Yes, AI can do that. But unique, expressive, story-driven music? That still requires a human soul. AI is a tool. It helps musicians work faster, not disappear.

So here’s our conclusion.

AI is not the final artist. It has no heart, no story, no intention. But it is an incredibly powerful assistant. The best music of the future won’t be AI alone, nor human alone – it will be **AI and human**.  

Thank you.
